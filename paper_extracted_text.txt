Abstract
Security Operations Centers routinely face a large volume of phishing and social engineering threats that exploit human cognitive vulnerabilities rather than
traditional software flaws. Existing security awareness training platforms rely on static email templates and simple click-tracking mechanisms that fail to simulate
how employees evaluate persuasive pretexts under varying operational pressures. This paper presents SemanticShield AI, an intelligent agentic platform designed for
automated phishing simulation and behavioral threat analysis. Powered by a FastAPI backend, a React and Vite dashboard, SQLAlchemy storage, and live Google
Gemini language models streaming over Server-Sent Events, SemanticShield AI evaluates employee decision-making across four defined psychological profiles:
trusting, security-conscious, overwhelmed, and executive. The platform passes agent reasoning vectors into a composite Risk Engine that applies weighted multi-
agent scoring (40% semantic analysis, 25% behavioral analysis, 35% URL intelligence) and high-risk floor enforcement, automatically creating simulation-tagged
incident records and broadcasting real-time telemetry over WebSockets. Empirical evaluation across 84 live simulation runs demonstrates that profile prompt
engineering yields high behavioral fidelity: the trusting profile exhibited a 100.0% vulnerability rate across malicious scenarios, whereas the security-conscious
profile achieved a 0.0% vulnerability rate (100.0% safe decisions). The engine achieved distinct discrete risk scoring of 84 for vulnerable outcomes and 18 for safe
outcomes with zero fallback execution failures across all evaluated live API runs.
Keywords— Security Operations Center, Phishing Simulation, Behavioral Threat Analysis, Agentic AI, Large Language Models, FastAPI, React, Risk Engine, Synthetic
Testing.
1 Introduction
The volume and sophistication of phishing, spear-phishing, and social
engineering attacks targeting modern organizations have grown to
unprecedented levels. Adversaries frequently bypass traditional perimeter
defenses such as email security gateways, Web Application Firewalls, and
intrusion detection systems by leveraging legitimate infrastructure, brand
impersonation, urgent executive pretexting, and credential harvesting
landing pages. While technical controls continuously evolve to block
malicious payloads, human employees remain the primary targets of initial
access techniques as documented under MITRE ATT&CK technique T1566.
Traditional Security Information and Event Management platforms,
Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response playbooks, and security
awareness training tools suffer from significant structural limitations.
Conventional phishing awareness software relies on static, pre-written email
templates with static click-tracking links. These systems do not simulate the
interactive cognitive decision-making of employees under varying
operational conditions, nor do they provide Security Operations Center
analysts with explainable, automated risk scoring derived from behavioral
telemetry.
This paper introduces SemanticShield AI, an intelligent agentic
framework designed to bridge the gap between offensive social engineering
simulation and defensive SOC monitoring. SemanticShield AI integrates
dynamic LLM-based phishing simulation, multi-agent behavioral threat
analysis, deterministic composite risk scoring, incident tracking, and real-
time WebSocket delivery into a unified application.
A central design principle behind SemanticShield AI is the separation of
generative AI reasoning from deterministic risk calculation. Artificial
intelligence is employed to simulate employee cognitive processes and
generate explainable investigation narratives, while composite risk scoring
and security overrides are executed through verified, deterministic code
rules inside the Risk Engine. This design ensures that LLM outputs remain
grounded in verified security attributes while human analysts retain full
control over operational response actions.
The platform incorporates synthetic testing controls, ensuring that all
phishing simulations operate in a controlled sandbox environment. No actual
email messages are dispatched over public networks, eliminating risk to live
corporate messaging infrastructure while allowing security teams to
benchmark vulnerability across diverse employee personas.

2 Limitations of Existing Systems
Modern digital services generate vast streams of network traffic,
authentication logs, and security telemetry, making manual monitoring
impossible. A single log entry rarely reveals an ongoing campaign on its
own; a failed login may be benign, but repeated failures followed by an
immediate password reset request may signal an active account takeover
attempt. Security Operations Centers rely on SIEMs, intrusion detection
systems, and threat intelligence feeds to aggregate alerts, but enterprise SOC
tooling remains complex, fragmented, and prohibitively expensive for small
security teams, educational institutions, and research laboratories.
In existing systems, phishing simulation and incident monitoring are
treated as completely disconnected functions. Phishing awareness tools send
static batch emails and record binary open/click metrics without assessing
why an employee fell for a specific pretext or how different job roles
process deceptive signals. Conversely, defensive monitoring tools ingest
telemetry without visibility into human vulnerability patterns across
departments. This separation forces analysts to manually reconcile data
across disparate dashboards, delaying incident triage and leaving
organizations exposed to multi-stage social engineering campaigns.
Furthermore, traditional security awareness training fails to account for
employee stress, workload pressure, and organizational authority dynamics.
A finance manager handling urgent payment deadlines is significantly more
likely to click a deceptive wire transfer link than an off-duty software
developer carefully inspecting URL parameters. Static templates cannot
model these psychological profiles dynamically, producing unrealistic
awareness metrics that give security teams a false sense of protection.

3 System Architecture
SemanticShield AI is structured around a modular, three-tier architecture
designed to separate presentation, business logic, and database persistence
while ensuring real-time telemetry delivery.
3.1 Three-Tier Architecture
Figure 1 depicts the three-tier architecture of SemanticShield AI across the
Presentation, Application, and Data tiers. The presentation layer is built with
React, Vite, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS, offering an interactive
simulation sandbox, defensive scanning portal, incident dashboard, and
analytics view. The application tier comprises a FastAPI backend hosting the
Phishing Simulation Engine, Gemini LLM Handler, Risk Engine, Incident
Manager, Activity Feed Manager, and WebSocket Manager. The data tier
uses SQLAlchemy ORM backed by SQLite storage for local operational
deployment.
Figure 1: SemanticShield AI system architecture: Presentation Tier (React + Vite
frontend), Application Tier (FastAPI backend, Risk Engine, Phishing Simulation
Engine), and Data Tier (SQLAlchemy ORM SQLite storage).
3.2 Data Flow
Figure 2 details the genuine data flow diagram of SemanticShield AI.
Requests originated by the Security Admin or automated testing scripts enter
Process 1.0 (Simulation Ingestion), where incoming scenario payloads and
target role specifications are validated. Process 2.0 (Gemini LLM Agent
Execution) constructs behavioral system prompts and streams reasoning text
over Server-Sent Events. Process 3.0 (Risk Scoring & Classification) passes
multi-agent payload vectors to the Risk Engine, which computes a
composite score. Process 4.0 (Telemetry & WebSocket Broadcast) streams
updates to connected clients while persisting records into Data Store D1
(SQLite database).
Figure 2: SemanticShield AI data flow diagram: security simulation payloads move
from external entities through ingestion, Gemini LLM execution, risk scoring,
telemetry broadcast, and database persistence.
3.3 Use Case
Figure 3 illustrates the primary use case interactions within SemanticShield
AI. Three primary actors interact with the system boundary: the Security
Administrator / SOC Analyst, the AI Agent (Gemini LLM), and the
Simulated Employee. The Security Admin executes interactive simulation
tests, configures scenario parameters, monitors incident queues, and reviews
behavioral analytics. The Gemini AI Agent streams step-by-step reasoning
and extracts decision tags. The Simulated Employee profile defines the
psychological context under which the email payload is evaluated.

4 Module-by-Module Implementation
The core processing pipeline of SemanticShield AI is implemented as a
modular twelve-step workflow. Each module performs a single, well-defined
security or processing function, ensuring clean separation of concerns across
the application.
4.1 Input Validation and Ingestion
Input validation and scenario ingestion handle incoming HTTP requests
submitted to the phishing simulation endpoints. The backend validates
required parameters including scenario name, employee role, behavioral
profile, email subject, and email body, assigning a unique simulation
identifier and UTC timestamp before forwarding the payload to prompt
selection.
4.2 Behavioral Profile Selection
Behavioral profile selection retrieves the system prompt corresponding to
the target employee persona. Four distinct psychological profiles are
defined: trusting, security-conscious, overwhelmed, and executive. Each
profile

4.3 Agent Simulation and Streaming
Agent simulation and LLM streaming construct the structured user message
and establish a streaming connection with the Google GenAI SDK. The
handler passes system instructions and email content to live Gemini models,
streaming reasoning text chunks back to the client over Server-Sent Events
with automatic model failover and exponential retry logic on rate limits.

4.4 Decision Extraction
Decision extraction parses the accumulated reasoning text using regular
expressions to locate explicit decision tags. The parser searches for
bracketed indicators signifying whether the simulated employee yielded to
the attack or rejected the email, falling back to keyword heuristics if
required.
4.5 Risk Scoring and Categorization
Composite risk scoring and categorization pass multi-agent output vectors to
the Risk Engine. The engine applies weighted averaging across semantic
analysis (40%), behavioral analysis (25%), and URL intelligence (35%),
enforcing a mandatory risk floor when any single agent score exceeds
critical threshold boundaries.
4.6 Incident Creation and Tracking
Incident creation and management convert high-risk simulation outcomes
into structured incident records. The manager populates threat severity,
classification, affected roles, and MITRE ATT&CK technique mappings,
tagging records as synthetic simulations to preserve operational separation
from live defense incidents.
4.7 Real-Time Telemetry Broadcast
Real-time telemetry and WebSocket broadcast dispatch updated simulation
results, activity feed logs, and incident notifications to all connected
frontend clients without requiring manual browser page refreshes.

6 Design-versus-Implementation Review
In the manner of a rigorous source-level engineering review, this section
evaluates how the implemented SemanticShield AI system corresponds to
the architectural design and workflow specifications. The review focuses on
the continuous processing path, separation between LLM simulation and
deterministic risk scoring, real-time delivery, and analyst-controlled
operations.
6.1 The Continuous Simulation Pipeline
The system design specifies a continuous workflow connecting scenario
submission, streaming reasoning, decision extraction, risk scoring, incident
creation, and dashboard rendering. The implemented runtime path directly
matches this design: requests received by FastAPI pass through validation,

6.2 Behavioral Simulation versus Deterministic Risk Scoring
A key architectural boundary separates probabilistic LLM reasoning from
deterministic risk calculation. Generative AI is utilized exclusively to
simulate employee psychological reactions and explain decision rationales,
whereas final risk scoring is governed entirely by deterministic code rules
inside Risk Engine. This design prevents LLM hallucination from distorting
enterprise risk scores.
6.3 Risk Aggregation and Incident Creation
The architecture requires automatic transformation of vulnerable simulation
outcomes into actionable incidents. The implemented Incident Manager
successfully instantiates structured incident objects containing unique
identifiers, scenario details, severity levels, and MITRE T1566.001/002
technique tags, satisfying the requirement to convert isolated events into
managed SOC records.
6.4 Real-Time Delivery versus Polling
The design specifies event-driven telemetry distribution. Functional testing
confirms that completed simulations emit WebSocket events on dedicated
channels, allowing the React frontend dashboard, incidents table, and
activity feed to update dynamically without requiring manual page reloads.
6.5 Analyst-Controlled and Synthetic-Only Operation
The platform enforces strict operational guardrails: all simulations are
entirely synthetic, and no actual email messages are ever transmitted over
external networks. Furthermore, defensive response actions remain strictly
under human analyst control.
6.6 What Is Verified Working
System verification confirmed complete end-to-end functionality across all
core pipeline modules. A total of 84 live simulation runs were successfully
executed using live Google Gemini API models with zero fallback generator
executions. All 28 scenario-profile combinations were evaluated across 3
repetitions, risk scores of 84 and 18 were correctly calculated, SQLite
persistence was verified after full backend server restarts, and production
frontend builds completed clean with zero errors.
7 Limitations and Future Work
While SemanticShield AI successfully demonstrates dynamic behavioral
phishing simulation, several technical limitations suggest directions for
future enhancement:
Single-LLM Dependence: The current implementation relies primarily on
Google Gemini family models. Integrating multi-model provider fallbacks
(e.g., Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT-4, Llama 3) will improve resilience
against provider outages.
Simulated vs Human Behavior: Simulations rely on prompt-engineered
LLM personas. Future research should benchmark LLM profile decisions
against empirical human participant behavior in real-world cohort studies.
Local Development Deployment: The current storage uses SQLite.
Future production deployments should migrate to PostgreSQL with
containerized orchestration.
Containerized Stack Verification: Docker Compose stacks require full
verification under production container networking environments.
Access Control & Authentication: Role-based access control and multi-
factor authentication should be implemented for administrative endpoints.
API Rate Limiting: Public simulation endpoints require per-client rate
limiting to prevent API quota exhaustion.
Multi-Model Comparison: Comparative analysis across multiple LLM
parameter scales should be conducted to evaluate prompt adherence
variance.
Validation with Human Participants: User studies are needed to refine
prompt characteristics against real employee security awareness
benchmarks.
Adaptive Scenario Generation: Future iterations will incorporate
dynamic scenario generation based on organizational threat intelligence
feeds.

9 Conclusion
This paper presented SemanticShield AI, an intelligent agentic platform for
behavioral phishing simulation and threat analysis. By integrating profile-
driven LLM prompt engineering, multi-agent composite risk scoring,
incident management, and real-time WebSocket telemetry into a unified
architecture, SemanticShield AI demonstrates how agentic AI can enhance
cybersecurity training and SOC monitoring. Experimental evaluation across
84 live simulation runs confirmed distinct behavioral profiling, high
decision consistency across repetitions, robust composite risk scoring, and
zero fallback execution failures. SemanticShield AI offers a scalable,
explainable foundation for security awareness research and modern Security
Operations Center automation.
